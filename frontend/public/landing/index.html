How it works

One timeline. Every second accounted for.

Prediction comes from measured features — pacing, repetition, new information, visual change and promise payoff. The language model only explains the evidence; it never invents it.

  1. 01

    Predict

    Upload a script or a cut. Every 10–15 second segment is measured, then turned into a retention-risk curve.

  2. 02

    Explain

    Click any dip to see why. Each flag cites the evidence and the exact range: “Repeats 01:10–01:30”, “Intro runs 45 s before the hook”.

  3. 03

    Fix

    Get the edit, not advice: cut 02:03–02:20, move the demo to 02:21, add a hook line. Every action points to a timestamp.

  4. 04

    Simulate

    Apply the edit virtually and rerun the model. Original and simulated curves sit side by side — always labelled as a model estimate.

Beyond the curve

From flag to finished edit.

Everything below runs on your own machine, works in English, Hindi and Hinglish, and labels every estimate as one.

  • Promise ledger

    Every promise the opening makes, tracked to its payoff: kept on time, kept late, or never kept. “Demo promised at 00:34, shown at 02:30.”

  • Show me what to cut

    The smallest range that removes the problem, snapped to pauses between words. Build an edit plan with cuts, trims and speed changes.

  • Render the edit

    FFmpeg renders an edited copy and plays it next to the original, each with its predicted curve. Your original file is never touched.

  • Rewrite, with a meaning check

    A local model tightens the flagged lines in your language. Rewrites that change the meaning, add numbers or don’t get shorter are refused.

  • Hook A/B simulator

    Paste two versions of your opening and compare their simulated curves before you record. A simulation, not a real A/B test, and labelled that way.

  • Delivery evidence

    Flat pitch against your own range, long pauses between words, phrases said again and again: added as evidence on the flags they explain.

  • Creator report

    Top issues, exact edits and validation numbers on one printable page, ready to hand to your editor as a PDF.

  • Private by default

    Uploads are encrypted at rest, deleted on a schedule or on request, and no transcript leaves the machine for a third-party AI service.

Validation

Tested on real viewing data. Misses included.

The model is trained and tested on real in-video drop-off from 310,360 viewers of online-course lectures, held out by lecture. On 220 unseen lectures it beats the average-curve baseline on curve error (0.172 vs 0.220) and drop precision (0.48 vs 0.41), and catches fewer drops (1,100 vs 1,306 of 1,660). Asked to name drop points, a plain LLM found 63 of 296 real drops; DROPZERO found 99.
